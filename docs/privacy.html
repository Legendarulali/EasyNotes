Privacy Policy

Effective November 27, 2026 · Applies to the EasyNotes app on every platform and to this website.

In short
  • Your notes are stored on your device. You don't need an account.
  • EasyNotes has no servers, no analytics, no ads and no tracking.
  • Google sign-in is optional. It is used only to back up your notebooks to a folder in your own Google Drive, and to bring them back on a new device.
  • The app asks only for the drive.file permission. It can see the files it created there, and nothing else in your Drive.
  • You can sign out, revoke access or delete the backup at any time.

Who we are

EasyNotes is a free, open-source note-taking app published by its developer (GitHub: Legendarulali) under the MIT licence. The full source code is public at github.com/Legendarulali/EasyNotes, so you can check everything in this policy yourself. In this policy, “we” means the developer of EasyNotes.

Data stored on your device

Everything you create in EasyNotes stays on your device: notebooks, whiteboards, handwritten strokes, sticky notes, imported PDFs, folders and page thumbnails. On desktop it is kept in the EasyNotes folder of your Documents folder; on Android and iPad it is kept in the app's private storage. PDFs you export go to your Downloads folder on desktop. On Android and iPad they are saved in the app's storage and handed to the system share sheet, so you decide where they go next.

This data never leaves your device unless you turn on Google Drive backup. We cannot see it, and nobody sends it to us.

Optional Google sign-in and Drive backup

If you choose Sign in with Google, EasyNotes asks Google for one permission:

  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.file: “See, edit, create and delete only the specific Google Drive files you use with this app.” EasyNotes cannot read, list or change any other file in your Google Drive.

With that permission, the app:

  • creates a folder named EasyNotes in your My Drive and copies your library into it, keeping the same folders and files;
  • runs the backup every 3 minutes while you work, when you close a notebook tab or return to the library, after you rename, move or delete something, when the app goes to the background, and just before the app closes;
  • uploads only files that changed (it compares MD5 checksums that Google Drive reports), and moves backed-up copies of things you deleted to your Google Drive trash;
  • downloads the backup only to restore it: when you sign in and your library on this device is empty (a new device or a reinstall), the app copies the notebooks and folders of the backup back into it. A library that already has notebooks is never overwritten. The backup is never analysed or read for any other purpose.

To show you which account is signed in, the app reads your Google account's name, email address and profile photo link. On Windows and Linux they come from the Google Drive API (about.get, using the same drive.file permission); on Android, iOS and macOS, Google's sign-in SDK provides them. They are displayed in the app's account menu and kept on your device with your session. They are not used for anything else, and they are never sent anywhere except back to Google.

How your sign-in is stored

  • Windows: the session (Google's refresh token plus the account name, email and photo link) is encrypted with Windows DPAPI, so only your Windows user account can decrypt it.
  • Linux: the session is stored in a file that only your user account can read or write.
  • Android, iOS and macOS: sign-in is handled by Google's own sign-in SDK and the platform's secure storage.

The app also keeps a small local record (.backup-state.json in your library folder) of which files it has already backed up and their Drive file IDs, so it can skip files that haven't changed.

Where data goes

EasyNotes connects to Google's sign-in and Google Drive services (accounts.google.com, oauth2.googleapis.com, www.googleapis.com) only while you are signed in or signing in. Apart from that, it checks at most once a day whether a newer version has been released, by asking GitHub's public API for the latest release (api.github.com/repos/Legendarulali/EasyNotes/releases/latest). That request contains no notes, no account details and no identifier, just a generic app name. Like any web request it reveals your IP address to GitHub, as described in the GitHub Privacy Statement. If you are offline, the check is silently skipped. All of these connections use HTTPS. The desktop sign-in opens your web browser at Google's page and receives the answer on a temporary local address on your own computer (127.0.0.1).

There is no EasyNotes server. We don't receive, store, sell, rent or share your notes or your Google data, and we don't use them for advertising or to train AI or machine-learning models. Google processes the data in your Drive under the Google Privacy Policy.

Google API Services User Data Policy

EasyNotes' use and transfer of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Google user data is used only to provide the backup and restore feature that you can see in the app. No person reads it, and it is never transferred to anyone else.

Analytics, cookies and this website

The app has no analytics, crash reporting, advertising or tracking SDKs. This website has no cookies, no analytics, no tracking scripts and no third-party fonts. It is hosted on GitHub Pages, and GitHub may log basic request data (such as IP addresses) for security and operations, as described in the GitHub Privacy Statement. The download and source links lead to GitHub.

Your choices and control

  • Use it without an account. Every feature except the backup works offline, without signing in.
  • Sign out. Use Sign out in the account menu. On Windows and Linux this deletes the stored session from your device and asks Google to revoke the app's access. On Android, iOS and macOS it signs out through Google's SDK.
  • Revoke access. Remove EasyNotes at any time from your Google Account at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Delete your backup. Delete the EasyNotes folder from your Google Drive. Signing out does not delete it, so your backup stays yours.
  • Delete local data. Delete notebooks in the app, delete the EasyNotes folder in Documents, or uninstall the app.

Since we hold no copy of your data, there is nothing on our side to request, export or delete. Everything is on your device and, if you chose, in your Google Drive.

Security

Your data stays on your device and in your own Google account. Data is sent only to Google, over HTTPS, and sign-in sessions are protected as described above. No system is perfectly secure, so please keep your device and your Google account protected (for example with a screen lock and 2-Step Verification).

Children

EasyNotes collects no personal information for us, from anyone of any age. Google sign-in follows Google's own age requirements for Google Accounts.

Changes to this policy

If the app's handling of data changes, we will update this page and its effective date before the new version is released. The history of this page is public in the project's GitHub repository.
